# 得到品控手册写作与审稿标准

Use this reference as an additional quality bar for WeChat long-form writing, especially knowledge essays, policy interpretation, industry/company analysis, expert interview processing, tool-method articles, and personal learning reflections.

This is an operational condensation of `得到品控手册11.0_得到知识管理部.pdf`, not a full copy of the manual. Apply the principles; do not quote or reproduce the manual unless the user explicitly asks.

## Core Standard

得到式品控的核心不是让文章更漂亮，而是让读者完成一次清楚的认知迁移:

- From a real problem to a usable answer.
- From old understanding to new understanding.
- From scattered material to one clear takeaway.
- From abstract knowledge to something the reader can recognize, remember, and use.

## Writing Principles

1. User value first
   - Before writing, define who the article serves and what the reader can take away.
   - Do not treat "I want to explain this topic" as enough. Ask: what can the reader do, judge, avoid, or understand better after reading?

2. One article, one real challenge
   - A good article starts from a challenge, not a broad concept.
   - A challenge should include: subject, goal, and constraint.
   - Avoid five weak challenges in one article. For a normal WeChat article, deliver one main problem well.

3. Viewpoint is a migration path
   - A viewpoint is not just a sentence. It moves the reader from an old cognition to a new cognition.
   - Make the old view visible, then show why the new view is better, deeper, or more useful.
   - Avoid information piles that never produce a central judgment.

4. Structure manages attention
   - Do not rely on jokes, decoration, or random stories to hold attention.
   - Use structure: challenge -> old solution or misunderstanding -> new solution/view -> evidence -> application -> conclusion.
   - Each section should advance the reader, not repeat the same point with different wording.

5. Materials are curated, not piled up
   - Every case, data point, quote, screenshot, metaphor, or story must perform a job.
   - Good material is concrete and perceptible. Prefer people, scenes, numbers, decisions, processes, and consequences over abstract labels.
   - If a material does not support the core challenge or viewpoint, cut it.

6. Translate expert knowledge into user perspective
   - Professional material is not enough. Convert it into what the reader needs to know, why it matters, and how it changes their judgment.
   - For complex subjects, add dimensions and switch viewpoints: user, practitioner, system, history, future, region, institution, and stakeholder.

7. Avoid turning analysis into popular-science exposition
   - Explaining what something is is not enough.
   - Push the reader's cognition forward: what changed, what misunderstanding is corrected, what decision becomes clearer?

8. Avoid soft article and praise-report writing
   - For company, project, city, institution, or tool articles, stand with the reader, not with the subject being written about.
   - Do not only write "what it did well". Ask: what problem did it solve, what boundary does it have, what can others learn, and where might it fail?
   - Ask about mistakes, tradeoffs, abandoned paths, and process, not only achievements.

9. End with service, not flourish
   - A good ending is not merely literary.
   - It should summarize, extend, migrate the insight to another scene, give an action, or create a future use case for the reader.

10. Stable quality control
   - 品控 is not a final proofreading ritual. It is an awareness across topic selection, research, drafting, editing, formatting, and feedback.
   - User feedback must be converted into the next round of process improvement.

## Dedao Quality Review Checklist

Run this before final delivery for important articles:

- Reader: Is the target reader concrete enough?
- Challenge: Is there one clear challenge with subject, goal, and constraint?
- User value: Can the reader say "this helps me because..." within the first few paragraphs?
- Cognitive migration: What old view is being replaced or upgraded?
- Core viewpoint: Can the article be summarized as one memorable judgment?
- Structure: Does each section push the argument forward?
- Evidence: Are claims supported by cases, data, scenes, named facts, or clearly marked assumptions?
- Materials: Does every example have a function?
- Sensory detail: Are there enough concrete scenes, numbers, actions, or moments?
- Boundary: Does the article explain where the method or judgment applies and where it does not?
- Professional translation: Are expert terms translated into reader-facing language?
- Anti-soft-article: Does the piece avoid becoming a praise report, brand ad, or institutional self-introduction?
- Anti-popular-science: Does the piece deliver cognition, not only facts?
- Ending: Does the ending give summary, migration, action, or a reusable scene?
- Fact safety: Are uncertain facts verified, removed, or clearly marked?

## Common Failure Patterns

- Concept as topic: "What is X?" without a reader challenge.
- Phenomenon as challenge: listing many events but never extracting the real problem.
- Multiple challenges: trying to answer too many questions in one 3000-word article.
- No old view: the article states a new view without showing what it improves.
- Abstract material: using too many concepts and too few concrete moments.
- Soft article drift: praising a company, city, policy, or tool without extracting reusable knowledge.
- Data without interpretation: listing numbers but not telling the reader what the numbers change.
- Ending as slogan: closing with emotion but no cognitive service.

## How The Writing Team Applies This

- Editor-in-chief defines the reader, challenge, and cognition migration before drafting.
- Researcher checks evidence quality, source reliability, and missing context.
- Drafter writes around one challenge rather than a topic encyclopedia.
- Editor checks structure and section function.
- Ruthless editor cuts repeated setup and materials without a job.
- Plain-language translator converts expert knowledge into reader language.
- 得到品控官 runs the checklist above before final delivery.
